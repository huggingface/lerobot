# 2. Teleoperate and add cameras

**Goal:** control your robot with the leader arm, then add cameras and see what they see. **Time:** about 30 minutes. **You need:** your calibrated robot and leader arm from [step 1](./setup_your_robot), and one or two USB cameras.

> [!TIP]
> **Key idea:** to [teleoperate](./glossary#teleoperation) is to control the robot yourself, live: you move the leader arm and the follower copies it. The cameras are the policy's eyes: later, it will only know what they show.

## Step 1: Teleoperate your robot

Put the follower arm in a safe position, with nothing in its way, and run:

<!-- prettier-ignore-start -->
<hfoptions id="robot">
<hfoption id="SO-101">

**SO-101:** use your ports and the IDs from step 1.

```bash
lerobot-teleoperate \
    --robot.type=so101_follower \
    --robot.port=/dev/ttyACM0 \
    --robot.id=my_follower \
    --teleop.type=so101_leader \
    --teleop.port=/dev/ttyACM1 \
    --teleop.id=my_leader
```

</hfoption>
<hfoption id="Other robots">

**Other robots:** replace each `YOUR_...` value with your robot's settings from [step 1](./setup_your_robot#your-robots-settings), and use the IDs you calibrated with.

```bash
lerobot-teleoperate \
    --robot.type=YOUR_ROBOT_TYPE \
    --robot.port=YOUR_ROBOT_PORT \
    --robot.id=my_follower \
    --teleop.type=YOUR_TELEOP_TYPE \
    --teleop.port=YOUR_TELEOP_PORT \
    --teleop.id=my_leader
```

</hfoption>
</hfoptions>
<!-- prettier-ignore-end -->

Move the leader arm slowly: the follower copies every movement, gripper included. Take a few minutes to get used to it, for example by picking up an object. You'll do the same thing many times when you record.

Press <kbd>Ctrl</kbd> + <kbd>C</kbd> to stop.

> [!WARNING]
> When you stop, the follower's motors turn off and the arm goes limp. Hold it, or move it to a resting position with the leader arm, before you stop.

If an arm isn't calibrated yet, `lerobot-teleoperate` runs the calibration first. If it asks you to calibrate an arm you already calibrated, the ID doesn't match: use the exact same ID as in step 1.

## Step 2: Find your cameras

Plug in your cameras and run:

```bash
lerobot-find-cameras opencv
```

It lists every camera with its index (`Id: 0`, `Id: 1`, …) and saves a test picture from each one in `outputs/captured_images`. Open the pictures to see which index is which camera.

> [!NOTE]
> Camera indexes can change when you restart your computer or replug a camera. If a camera suddenly shows the wrong view or won't open, run `lerobot-find-cameras opencv` again.

## Step 3: Place your cameras

The camera images are what your policy learns from, so a good camera setup matters more than a high resolution. Most setups use two cameras: one **top** camera that sees the whole workspace, and one **wrist** camera on the robot's gripper.

- **Light the scene evenly.** Use diffuse, consistent light and avoid direct sunlight, strong reflections and moving shadows. Lighting that differs between recording and running the policy can make it fail.
- **Fix the cameras in place.** Mount them rigidly and don't move them between recording sessions, or between recording and running the policy.
- **Frame the task.** The object, the gripper and the target should be visible in at least one camera. A good test: could you do the task yourself by only looking at the camera images?
- **Keep the resolution modest.** 640×480 at 30 frames per second is enough for most tasks, and keeps recording and training fast.

## Step 4: Teleoperate with cameras

Run teleoperation again, this time with your cameras. Each camera gets a name (here `top` and `wrist`) and its index from Step 2. `--display_data=true` opens a window that shows the camera images and the position of every joint, live:

<!-- prettier-ignore-start -->
<hfoptions id="robot">
<hfoption id="SO-101">

**SO-101:**

```bash
lerobot-teleoperate \
    --robot.type=so101_follower \
    --robot.port=/dev/ttyACM0 \
    --robot.id=my_follower \
    --robot.cameras="{ top: {type: opencv, index_or_path: 0, width: 640, height: 480, fps: 30}, wrist: {type: opencv, index_or_path: 1, width: 640, height: 480, fps: 30}}" \
    --teleop.type=so101_leader \
    --teleop.port=/dev/ttyACM1 \
    --teleop.id=my_leader \
    --display_data=true
```

</hfoption>
<hfoption id="Other robots">

**Other robots:**

```bash

    --robot.type=YOUR_ROBOT_TYPE \
    --robot.port=YOUR_ROBOT_PORT \
    --robot.id=my_follower \
    --robot.cameras="{ top: {type: opencv, index_or_path: 0, width: 640, height: 480, fps: 30}, wrist: {type: opencv, index_or_path: 1, width: 640, height: 480, fps: 30}}" \
    --teleop.type=YOUR_TELEOP_TYPE \
```

> [!IMPORTANT]
> Keep the same camera names and positions for the rest of the tutorial. The names are saved in your dataset (`observation.images.top`, `observation.images.wrist`), and your trained policy will expect exactly these cameras when it runs.

## Check it worked

- The follower arm copies the leader arm smoothly, gripper included.
- The window opened by `--display_data=true` shows both camera views, and the joint values change as you move the leader arm.
- In the camera views, you can see the object, the gripper and the target.

<details>
<summary>If something goes wrong</summary>

- **The follower moves in the wrong direction, or stops short of the leader:** calibrate both arms again ([step 1](./setup_your_robot#step-4-calibrate-both-arms)). See also [Troubleshooting](./troubleshooting#the-follower-doesnt-move-like-the-leader).
- **`Failed to open OpenCVCamera(0)`:** the index changed, or another app uses the camera. See [Troubleshooting](./troubleshooting#failed-to-open-a-camera).
- **The camera view is choppy, or `failed to set fps`:** use a resolution and frame rate that `lerobot-find-cameras opencv` lists for that camera, and plug the cameras into different USB ports.

</details>

<details>
<summary>Go further</summary>

- **Other teleoperation devices:** keyboard, gamepad, phone or VR headset, depending on the robot. See [Teleoperation devices](./teleoperators).
- **Depth cameras and other camera types:** Intel RealSense, network cameras and more are covered in [Cameras](./cameras).
- **Foxglove instead of Rerun:** add `--display_mode=foxglove` and connect the Foxglove app to `ws://127.0.0.1:8765`.
- **Teleoperate from Python:** the same loop in a few lines of code.

<!-- prettier-ignore-start -->
```python
import time

from lerobot.cameras.opencv import OpenCVCameraConfig
from lerobot.robots.so_follower import SO101Follower, SO101FollowerConfig
from lerobot.teleoperators.so_leader import SO101Leader, SO101LeaderConfig

robot = SO101Follower(SO101FollowerConfig(
    port="/dev/ttyACM0",
    id="my_follower",
    cameras={
        "top": OpenCVCameraConfig(index_or_path=0, width=640, height=480, fps=30),
        "wrist": OpenCVCameraConfig(index_or_path=1, width=640, height=480, fps=30),
    },
))
leader = SO101Leader(SO101LeaderConfig(port="/dev/ttyACM1", id="my_leader"))
robot.connect()
leader.connect()

while True:
    action = leader.get_action()        # where the leader arm is
    robot.send_action(action)           # the follower goes there
    observation = robot.get_observation()  # camera images and joint positions
    time.sleep(1 / 30)                     # about 30 times per second
```
