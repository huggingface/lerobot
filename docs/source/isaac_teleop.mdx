# Isaac Teleop

Control your robot with NVIDIA [Isaac Teleop](https://github.com/NVIDIA/IsaacTeleop), a
multi-modal teleoperation framework. Isaac Teleop drives a single `TeleopSession` from a range
of input devices — XR (VR) controllers, hand tracking, full-body tracking, Manus gloves, foot
pedals, and more.

An XR controller in hand drives the arm's end effector directly — squeeze to engage, move your
hand, and the arm follows; release and it slews back to its reset pose. No leader arm, no
calibration rig, and the same controller retargets across every supported follower.

The XR controller ships as a native teleoperator, `--teleop.type=isaac_teleop`
(`src/lerobot/teleoperators/isaac_teleop/`), usable directly with `lerobot-teleoperate` /
`lerobot-record` — no bespoke entry point:

```bash
lerobot-teleoperate --robot.type=so101_follower --robot.port=/dev/ttyACM0 --robot.id=black \
    --teleop.type=isaac_teleop --teleop.id=xr_controller
```

**In this guide you'll learn:**

- How the Isaac Teleop XR controller drives a robot end‑effector (EE) target
- How the _clutch_ (squeeze/grip on the XR controller) engages teleoperation without jerking the arm
- How to teleoperate and record with the SO‑101, and tune motion / gripper / IK

## Controls

- **Squeeze / grip** — the **clutch** (deadman) and the segment boundary. Hold it past
  `clutch_threshold` to engage teleoperation; release and the arm slews back to its reset pose,
  re-homing the clutch there. Reposition your hand during the slew — each engage re-captures the
  origin from the arm's measured pose, so it never jumps. `--teleop.reset_to_origin=false` keeps
  the arm where it is on release.
- **Wrist alignment** — a **precondition on that squeeze**, by default. Because the clutch composes
  orientation as a delta, latching at a misaligned wrist offsets the arm from the hand for the whole
  engagement, so an **engage gate** holds the latch until the controller's rotation is within
  `--teleop.engage_enter_deg` (default 20°) of the pose about to be latched
  (`--teleop.engage_exit_deg`, default 30°, to fall back out; `--teleop.engage_dwell_s`, default
  0.1s, dwell). The robot twin drawn in the headset **turns green** when it will latch, which is
  what makes the requirement perceivable rather than a squeeze that silently does nothing. A
  denied squeeze is _owed_, not lost: it fires on the first aligned frame.
  `--teleop.engage_gate=false` restores the old behaviour.
- **Trigger** — the **gripper**, controlled **analog**. The jaw tracks the trigger
  proportionally — a half-pressed trigger leaves the jaw half-closed.
- **Controller orientation** — the **wrist**. The clutch rebases the controller orientation
  (engage-relative, base-frame) into a soft IK orientation target the wrist tracks alongside
  position. On the 5-DOF SO-101 the wrist follows the hand only partially by design.

**Declutch is also the episode boundary when recording.** With `lerobot-record`, releasing the
squeeze (having engaged it at least once) ends the current episode via the same
`TeleopEvents.TERMINATE_EPISODE` mechanism `GamepadTeleop`/`KeyboardEndEffectorTeleop` use, then
the arm slews back to its reset pose. `--teleop.reset_to_origin=false` disables the slew (connect
and every declutch alike).

### The robot twin

`--teleop.robot_twin` (on by default, SO-101/SO-100 only) runs Isaac Teleop's `ClutchPreview`
stereoscopically in the same OpenXR session the controllers stream over. While disengaged you drag
an SO-101 around by hand and it turns green when the clutch will latch; on engage it is replaced by
a leader gripper locked to your hand, its jaw on your trigger, recolouring amber and red as the
safety limiter clamps and refuses.

It is anchored against the measured head pose and is **not registered to the physical arm** — a
preview you aim with, not an overlay on your workspace. Needs a Linux `isaacteleop` built with
`-DBUILD_VIZ=ON`; without one it warns and the loop runs unchanged.

## How it works

`XRController` drives the controller pose through Isaac Teleop's own in-pipeline clutch and
safety rate limiter, then runs LeRobot's closed-loop Cartesian IK internally — `get_action()`
returns a plain joint-space action like any other teleoperator, no bespoke loop or manual
pipeline wiring needed on the caller's side.

```text
  VR controller (OpenXR)
        │
        ▼
  Isaac Teleop's clutch + rate limiter   ── absolute base-frame EE pose + trigger + engaged
        │  (SO101ClutchRetargeter: engage-relative delta on the EE home, pos+orient;
        │   ClutchPreview: the robot twin/ghost, engage gate, safety harness colouring)
        ▼
  MapXRControllerActionToRobotAction     ── absolute ee.x/y/z; ee.w* = orientation rotvec target;
        │                                    ee.gripper_pos from the trigger
        ▼
  EEBoundsAndSafety                      ── workspace clip + per-frame step clamp (clamp+warn)
        │
        ▼
  InverseKinematicsEEToJoints            ── closed-loop Placo IK; position + soft-orientation
        │                                    (passes ee.gripper_pos → gripper.pos)
        ▼
  XRController.get_action() returns {motor}.pos -- a plain joint-space action
```

- All of the above runs **inside** `XRController.get_action()`, including the command gate and
  the reset-to-origin slew (see [Controls](#controls)).
- The follower's kinematics/IK profile is picked automatically from `--robot.type` (and
  `--robot.motor_family` where relevant) — no separate flag to set.

## Important pipeline steps and options

The clutch already produces an absolute base-frame pose, so the IK stage is a thin
**absolute-pose** path — there is no frame remap, no delta accumulation, and no
`EEReferenceAndDelta` stage. All of this runs inside `XRController.get_action()`:

- `MapXRControllerActionToRobotAction` is a stateless per-frame mapping from the clutch's output to
  the IK input contract. It writes the absolute base-frame position, encodes the absolute
  orientation as a rotvec target, and inverts the closedness into a motor gripper target
  (`RobotProfile.gripper_open`/`gripper_close` set the endpoints per arm).
- `EEBoundsAndSafety` clamps the EE to a workspace (`RobotProfile.ee_bounds`) and rate-limits
  per-frame jumps (`RobotProfile.max_ee_step_m`). The clutch's no-teleport keeps frames small, so
  this mostly catches transient controller tracking glitches. `RobotProfile.raise_on_ee_jump`
  (default `True`) raises out of the loop on an over-limit frame; set `False` on a profile to
  clamp and warn instead.
- `InverseKinematicsEEToJoints(initial_guess_current_joints=True, orientation_weight=...)` solves
  closed-loop Placo IK, seeding each solve from the measured joints. SO-101 is a 5-DOF arm, so the
  IK is position-dominant; a small `orientation_weight` (`RobotProfile.orientation_weight`) lets it
  softly track the orientation target so the wrist follows the hand, while the under-determined
  roll stays partial by design.

See the [Processors for Robots and Teleoperators](./processors_robots_teleop) guide for more on
how these processor steps compose, and `src/lerobot/teleoperators/isaac_teleop/robot_profiles.py`
for the full per-robot `RobotProfile` definitions.

## Troubleshooting

- **`ModuleNotFoundError: isaacteleop`** — the `isaacteleop` package is not installed in the
  active environment. Re-run the install command at the top of this guide:
  `uv pip install -e ".[isaac-teleop]"`.
- **Arm does not move** — the clutch is a deadman: you must hold the squeeze/grip past
  `clutch_threshold`. Lower the threshold if your controller's squeeze is reported softly.
- **Arm runs out of reach mid-motion** — your hand is out-travelling the arm. Lower
  `--teleop.clutch_position_scale` (default `0.5`) so the same sweep commands less EE travel, or
  release and re-engage the clutch to re-center.
- **Arm feels sluggish relative to your hand** — the opposite trade: raise
  `--teleop.clutch_position_scale` toward `1.0` for more EE travel per unit of hand motion, at the
  cost of reach headroom.
- **Motion feels misaligned** — confirm the headset/play space orientation. The controller stream
  is rebased into the robot base frame by the `--teleop.base_T_anchor` transform (default: standard
  OpenXR → robot axis convention; with a robot twin, `--teleop.align_frame_to_preview=true` — the
  default — re-measures its yaw continuously while disengaged and freezes it through each
  engagement instead of trusting the static default).
- **`robot.connect()` fails with a hardware-driver error unrelated to any of the above** (e.g. a
  motor-position/enable check) — that error comes from the follower robot's own driver
  (`motorbridge`, `feetech-servo-sdk`, etc.), not from Isaac Teleop or this teleoperator; it fires
  identically with any other `--teleop.type`. Diagnose it as you would for that robot on its own.
