# 5. Train a policy

**Goal:** train a [policy](./glossary#policy) on your dataset. **Time:** 1 to 8 hours depending on the GPU, mostly waiting. **You need:** the name of your dataset from [step 4](./check_dataset), and a GPU: your own, or one in the cloud.

> [!TIP]
> **Key idea:** training shows the policy the frames of your dataset again and again, until the actions it predicts match yours. Along the way it saves [checkpoints](./glossary#checkpoint), copies of the policy you can run. This tutorial trains [ACT](./act): a small, fast policy made to learn one task from about 50 episodes. It's the best first policy; [Choose a policy](./policies) compares the others.

## Step 1: Choose where to train

Training needs a GPU. Your robot isn't needed for this step, so you can train on any computer.

| Where                        | How                                                                            | Time for this tutorial |
| ---------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------ | ---------------------- |
| **Your own NVIDIA GPU**      | Add `--policy.device=cuda`. 8 GB of GPU memory is plenty for ACT.              | 30 minutes to 2 hours  |
| **A Mac with Apple Silicon** | Add `--policy.device=mps` (macOS 15 or newer).                                 | 6 to 14 hours          |
| **Google Colab**             | Free GPU in your browser: follow the [ACT notebook](./notebooks#training-act). | 2 to 4 hours           |
| **Hugging Face Jobs**        | A rented cloud GPU, paid per second: see [Step 3](#step-3-start-training).     | 1 to 2 hours           |

These times are for about 30,000 training steps of ACT. See [Which GPU do I need?](./hardware_guide) for other GPUs and policies.

## Step 2: Choose how long to train

Training length is counted in **steps**: one step learns from a small batch of frames, 8 by default. One pass over the whole dataset is an **epoch**. Policies like ACT usually learn a task in 5 to 10 epochs.

Your 50 episodes of about 30 seconds at 30 frames per second hold about 45,000 frames, so one epoch is about 5,600 steps. **30,000 steps** (about 5 epochs) is a good first run. The default is 100,000 steps, which is more than a small dataset needs. For other dataset sizes, see [how to calculate the number of steps](./hardware_guide#training-time).

## Step 3: Start training

Run this on the computer with the GPU. Use the name of your cleaned dataset from step 4 (or the full name of the original if you didn't remove any episodes):

```bash
lerobot-train \
    --dataset.repo_id=${HF_USER}/my_first_dataset_clean \
    --policy.type=act \
    --policy.device=cuda \
    --policy.repo_id=${HF_USER}/my_first_policy \
    --steps=30000 \
    --save_freq=10000 \
    --output_dir=outputs/train/my_first_policy \
    --job_name=my_first_policy \
    --trackio.enable=true
```

What the options mean:

- `--dataset.repo_id`: the dataset to learn from.
- `--policy.type=act`: train a new ACT policy. It adapts automatically to your robot's joints and cameras, as saved in the dataset.
- `--policy.device`: `cuda` for an NVIDIA GPU, `mps` for a Mac.
- `--policy.repo_id`: where the trained policy is uploaded on the Hub when training ends.
- `--steps` and `--save_freq`: train for 30,000 steps, and save a checkpoint every 10,000.
- `--output_dir` and `--job_name`: where the checkpoints are saved on your computer, and the name of the run.
- `--trackio.enable=true`: log training curves to [Trackio](https://huggingface.co/docs/trackio), which you can watch in your browser (see Step 4).

**On Hugging Face Jobs**, the same training runs on a rented cloud GPU. Add `--job.target` with the GPU you want, here an A10G:

```bash
lerobot-train \
    --dataset.repo_id=${HF_USER}/my_first_dataset_clean \
    --policy.type=act \
    --policy.repo_id=${HF_USER}/my_first_policy \
    --steps=30000 \
    --save_freq=10000 \
    --job.target=a10g-small
```

Jobs are billed per second of use: `hf jobs hardware` lists the GPUs and their prices. The training logs stream to your terminal, and the policy is uploaded to `${HF_USER}/my_first_policy` when the job ends. Pressing <kbd>Ctrl</kbd> + <kbd>C</kbd> only stops showing the logs: the job keeps running in the cloud (see "Go further" below to follow or cancel it).

## Step 4: Watch training

Every 200 steps, the training log prints a line with the current `step` and `loss`. The **loss** measures how far the policy's actions are from yours: it should drop quickly at first, then flatten out.

With `--trackio.enable=true`, open the training curves in your browser with:

```bash
trackio show --project lerobot
```

Checkpoints are saved in `outputs/train/my_first_policy/checkpoints/`, in one folder per save (named after the step) and in `last`, which always points to the newest one.

## Check it worked

- Training reached the last step without an error.
- `outputs/train/my_first_policy/checkpoints/last/pretrained_model` exists on the computer you trained on.
- Your policy has a page on the Hub: `https://huggingface.co/YOUR_HF_USERNAME/my_first_policy`.

> [!NOTE]
> Like datasets, policies are uploaded **publicly** by default. Add `--policy.private=true` to upload a private policy, or `--policy.push_to_hub=false` to keep it only on your computer.

<details>
<summary>If something goes wrong</summary>

- **`CUDA out of memory`:** lower the batch size with `--batch_size=4`. See [Troubleshooting](./troubleshooting#cuda-out-of-memory).
- **Training is very slow:** check that it uses the GPU. Run `lerobot-info` and look at `Is PyTorch built with CUDA support?`, and see [Training is slow, or PyTorch doesn't use the GPU](./troubleshooting#training-is-slow-or-pytorch-doesnt-use-the-gpu).
- **`'repo_id' argument missing`:** add `--policy.repo_id=${HF_USER}/my_first_policy`, or `--policy.push_to_hub=false` if you don't want to upload the policy.
- **Training stopped before the end:** resume it, see "Go further" below.

</details>

<details>
<summary>Go further</summary>

**Resume training** from the last checkpoint, with the same settings:

```bash
lerobot-train \
    --config_path=outputs/train/my_first_policy/checkpoints/last/pretrained_model/train_config.json \
    --resume=true
```

If the run uploaded its checkpoints to the Hub (with `--save_checkpoint_to_hub=true`), resume straight from there with `--config_path=${HF_USER}/my_first_policy --resume=true`. Adding `--job.target=a10g-small` to either command resumes on Hugging Face Jobs.

**Upload a checkpoint yourself**, for example if you stopped training early, or to keep an intermediate one:

```bash
hf upload ${HF_USER}/my_first_policy outputs/train/my_first_policy/checkpoints/last/pretrained_model
```

**Describe your policy on the Hub** with `--policy.tags='["so101","tutorial"]'` and `--policy.license=mit`.

**More about Hugging Face Jobs:**

- Follow or stop a job with `hf jobs logs JOB_ID` and `hf jobs cancel JOB_ID`.
- A job stops after 2 days. Change that with `--job.timeout`, for example `--job.timeout=4h`.
- If your dataset is only on your computer, it's uploaded to a **private** Hub repository first, so the job can read it.
- Add `--save_checkpoint_to_hub=true` to upload every checkpoint as it's saved, so you can follow progress during the run.
- Add your own tags to the job with `--job.tags='["my-tag"]'`.
- The policy's repository is created before training starts. If a job fails before uploading the policy, the empty repository stays on the Hub: delete it with `hf repos delete REPO_ID`.
- To log training curves from a job, pass `--trackio.space_id=${HF_USER}/lerobot-training`: the runs are saved in that Hugging Face Space.

**Train another policy:** [Choose a policy](./policies) compares them all. **Train faster on several GPUs:** see [Train on multiple GPUs](./multi_gpu_training).

</details>
