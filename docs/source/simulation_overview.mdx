# Simulation: when and why

A [simulation](./glossary#simulation) is a robot and its surroundings in software. **No robot? Start with [Train and evaluate in simulation](./train_in_sim):** it trains a policy in PushT, a small simulation that runs on any computer, and measures how well it works.

## What simulation is good for

- **Learning without a robot.** Record, train and evaluate the same way as on a real robot, for free, with nothing to build or set up.
- **Measuring and comparing policies.** The computer runs a policy many times from the same starting positions and checks each episode for success. The result is a [success rate](./glossary#success-rate), which you can compare between policies. A standard set of such tasks is a [benchmark](./glossary#benchmark): papers report their results on benchmarks such as LIBERO, so you can reproduce them.
- **Practising reinforcement learning.** A policy that learns by trial and error makes many mistakes first. In simulation, mistakes cost nothing.

## What it can't do

> [!WARNING]
> **A policy trained in simulation won't run on your real robot**, and a policy trained on your robot won't run in a simulation. The images, the robot, the objects and the physics are all different, and policies learn exactly what they saw. A high success rate in simulation also doesn't guarantee that the same kind of policy will work on your robot: always test there.

What does carry over is everything you learn about the workflow: recording, training, checkpoints and evaluating work the same way for real robots.

## All simulations

- **Install** gives the extra to add to LeRobot (for example `pip install 'lerobot[pusht]'`), or says when the simulation runs in Docker, through `lerobot-env-server`: see [Simulated robots and environments](./simulation#serve-an-environment).
- **Requirements** lists what the simulation's page says it needs. A dash means the page asks for nothing special.

## How simulations work in LeRobot

- **To evaluate a policy**, choose a simulation with `--env.type`, for example `--env.type=pusht`. `lerobot-eval` uses it, and so does `lerobot-train`, to evaluate the policy while it trains.
- **To use a simulation as a robot**, use `--robot.type=sim`. Then `lerobot-teleoperate`, `lerobot-record` and `lerobot-rollout` work with it as with a real robot.
- **Large simulations run in Docker**, so that their software doesn't conflict with LeRobot's. `lerobot-env-server` starts them, and LeRobot connects to them.

All the details are in [Simulated robots and environments](./simulation).
