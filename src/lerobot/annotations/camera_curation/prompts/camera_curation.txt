You are inspecting frames from ONE camera stream of a robot-manipulation dataset.
All frames come from the SAME camera during a single episode and are shown in
chronological order. Use the frames together to reason about the camera across
time; do not judge from a single frame.

Perform THREE tasks:

1. determine whether the camera is usable;
2. determine whether its viewpoint is FIXED or ROBOT_MOUNTED;
3. classify its physical viewpoint.

Return the result as one JSON object.

1. QUALITY

Decide whether this camera view is usable for training a robot policy.

A USABLE view must show a genuine camera view of the robot manipulation scene
clearly enough to learn from.

Mark it UNUSABLE if the frames do not clearly show the real scene because of:

  - severe blur or being out of focus;
  - severe over- or under-exposure;
  - corrupted frames;
  - a static/frozen camera feed;
  - a placeholder, "no camera", or "no signal" image;
  - a test pattern;
  - an application/logo graphic;
  - a blank, solid-colour, or nearly-empty image that does not depict the task;
  - a foreign obstruction blocking the lens such that no useful part of the task
    or robot is visible in any provided frame.

A real close-up is NOT an obstruction. If the work surface, manipulated object,
robot arm, gripper, or end effector fills most of the image, it is still a
genuine camera view and may be usable.

The robot's own arm, gripper, or end effector may partially block the workspace.
This is expected and MUST NOT by itself make the camera unusable.

Also mark the camera UNUSABLE if a human operator, human hand, or human arm is
PROMINENTLY or PERSISTENTLY visible in the working area.

A small or brief partial human intrusion at the edge of the image is tolerable.

Carefully distinguish human limbs from the robot's own manipulator.

Set "blur_reason" to a short reason for an unusable verdict, otherwise null.

2. MOUNT TYPE

Before assigning a view label, determine how the camera is mounted.

Choose exactly one:

  - "fixed"
  - "robot_mounted"
  - "unknown"

A ROBOT_MOUNTED camera is physically attached to the moving robot arm, wrist,
gripper, or end effector.

Use temporal evidence across the provided frames.

A robot-mounted camera exhibits EGOCENTRIC motion:

  - when the arm changes pose, the entire camera viewpoint changes with it;
  - the background/workspace undergoes viewpoint or parallax changes correlated
    with robot motion;
  - a visible part of the wrist/gripper may remain relatively stable with respect
    to the camera;
  - the workspace appears to move around the camera as the arm moves.

A FIXED camera keeps the same physical viewpoint of the scene:

  - the table, walls, fixtures, and other static scene geometry remain in
    approximately the same camera coordinate system;
  - the robot moves THROUGH the image;
  - the arm or gripper may move substantially within the image;
  - the overall viewpoint of the workspace does not travel with the robot.

IMPORTANT:

Seeing a gripper or robot arm is NOT evidence that the camera is robot-mounted.

Do NOT infer robot mounting merely because:

  - the gripper is visible;
  - the gripper occupies much of the image;
  - the camera is close to the manipulation;
  - the camera points approximately where the gripper points;
  - the robot arm partially blocks the workspace;
  - the view looks unusual or close-up.

If the static environment remains fixed while the robot moves through the image,
the camera is FIXED.

If there is insufficient temporal evidence to determine this reliably, use
"unknown".

3. VIEW LABEL

Choose a view label using ONLY this closed vocabulary:

  {vocabulary}

Classify hierarchically.

If mount_type == "robot_mounted"

The view label is:

  - "wrist"

Default to plain "wrist".

Only use "left_wrist" or "right_wrist" when there is explicit and reliable
evidence that:

  1. the robot has distinct left and right manipulators; AND
  2. this particular camera can reliably be associated with one of them.

Do NOT infer wrist handedness from:

  - which side of the image the arm appears on;
  - gripper orientation;
  - apparent left/right direction in one image;
  - image mirroring;
  - camera rotation.

If handedness is uncertain, use "wrist".

If mount_type == "fixed"

Classify primarily by the camera's PHYSICAL POSITION relative to the manipulation
workspace, using viewing direction and perspective as supporting evidence.

top

Use "top" when the camera is positioned predominantly ABOVE the manipulation
workspace and looks predominantly DOWN toward it.

The camera does NOT need to point perfectly vertically downward.

An elevated, oblique camera can still be "top" when its physical position is
primarily above the workspace.

Typical evidence includes:

  - a large amount of horizontal work surface is visible;
  - objects are substantially visible from above;
  - the view gives an overhead/global perspective of the manipulation area;
  - depth along the table plane appears relatively compressed.

Do NOT change a fixed camera from "top" to "wrist" merely because a robot arm or
gripper is prominent.

bottom

Use "bottom" when the camera is physically below the manipulation workspace and
looks upward toward it.

side views

A fixed camera positioned primarily OUTSIDE the workspace boundary and looking
INWARD toward the manipulation area is a side view.

This remains a side view even when the camera is elevated and therefore looks
somewhat downward.

Side views typically show:

  - stronger perspective depth across the workspace;
  - more vertical surfaces;
  - objects and robot geometry more from the side than from above.

When reliably identifiable, qualify where the camera physically sits relative to
the robot/workspace:

  - "front_side": camera in front, looking toward the robot/workspace;
  - "rear_side": camera behind, looking toward the robot/workspace;
  - "left_side": camera on the left;
  - "right_side": camera on the right.

Use plain "side" when it is clearly a side/external view but the direction cannot
be reliably established.

Do NOT infer left/right/front/rear merely from where the robot appears within the
image.

Camera images may be tilted, rolled, rotated, or mirrored. Reason about the
physical workspace geometry rather than image-frame edges.

If mount_type == "unknown"

Use "unknown" unless the view label can nevertheless be established with strong
visual evidence.

TOP VS SIDE — IMPORTANT

For elevated fixed cameras, do NOT classify solely from how much the camera
points downward.

Ask where the camera is most likely physically located relative to the workspace:

  - predominantly ABOVE the manipulation area -> "top";
  - predominantly OUTSIDE its front/rear/left/right boundary looking inward ->
    a side view.

A side camera can be high and look downward.
A top camera can be oblique.

Use the complete scene geometry across all provided frames to make this
distinction.

OUTPUT

{combo_rule}

Direction qualifiers must only be used with side/wrist labels. Do not invent
labels outside the vocabulary.

Return strictly valid JSON with exactly these keys:

  {{
    "usable": true,
    "blur_reason": null,
    "mount_type": "fixed",
    "view_label": "top",
    "confidence": 0.95
  }}

"confidence" must be a number between 0 and 1.

Return JSON only. No prose and no code fences.
