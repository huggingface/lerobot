You are inspecting frames from ONE camera of a robot manipulation dataset. All
frames come from the same fixed camera during a single episode; use them
together to judge the camera, not any single moment.

Do two things and return them as one JSON object.

1. QUALITY. Decide whether this camera view is usable for training a policy. A
   USABLE view must actually show the real robot and the workspace/objects it
   works on, clearly enough to learn from. Mark it UNUSABLE if EITHER:
   - it does not clearly show that real scene. This covers genuine capture
     defects (blurry / out of focus, badly over- or under-exposed, corrupted,
     static/frozen) AND frames that are not a real camera feed at all: a
     PLACEHOLDER or "no camera" / "no signal" icon, a test pattern, an app or
     logo graphic, or a blank / solid-colour / nearly-empty frame that does not
     depict the robot and its workspace. If the frame is not a genuine view of
     the robot scene, it is UNUSABLE. HOWEVER, a real close-up in which the work
     surface or a manipulated object fills most of the frame (a flat panel, a
     tabletop, the object being handled) is a GENUINE view — keep it usable; do
     NOT call the work surface itself an "obstruction". Only flag an obstruction
     when a FOREIGN object blocks the lens and NO part of the task or robot is
     visible in ANY of the frames shown; OR
   - a human is clearly part of the scene. These are teleoperated recordings and
     the operator should not be in the working area. Mark UNUSABLE when a person,
     or a human hand/arm, is PROMINENTLY or PERSISTENTLY in view — e.g. an
     operator reaching into the workspace through much of the episode. But a
     SMALL, PARTIAL intrusion at the edge of the frame — a hand briefly poking in
     from the side while the robot works — is tolerable and stays usable. Only
     the robot manipulator should occupy the workspace; do not mistake the
     robot's own gripper/arm for a human limb.
   Otherwise it is usable.

   Do NOT mark a view unusable because the robot's own gripper, arm, or
   end-effector is visible or partially blocks the view — that is EXPECTED,
   especially for a wrist-mounted camera where the gripper is normally in frame.
   Carefully distinguish the robot (a metal/plastic manipulator) from a human
   limb: the robot occluding its own workspace is fine; a human in frame is not.
   Set "blur_reason" to the reason for any UNUSABLE verdict (e.g. "out of focus",
   "human operator visible").

2. VIEW LABEL. Choose where this camera sits relative to the robot, using ONLY
   this closed vocabulary:

     {vocabulary}

   - side   : a FIXED camera viewing the robot from around it at working height
              (not overhead, not on the arm). Add a direction saying WHERE around
              the robot it sits: "front_side" (in front, facing the robot),
              "rear_side" (behind it, its back toward you), "left_side" or
              "right_side" (off to that side). Use plain "side" only if you
              cannot tell the direction.
   - top    : a FIXED camera above the workspace looking straight DOWN (overhead).
   - bottom : a FIXED camera below the workspace looking up.
   - wrist  : physically mounted ON the moving robot arm / wrist / end effector,
              so the WHOLE viewpoint travels with the arm (see the wrist rules
              below). Default to plain "wrist"; only add handedness with strong
              evidence (see "wrist handedness").

   WRIST-CAMERA RULES. Classify a camera as "wrist" ONLY when there is strong
   evidence it is physically mounted on the moving arm/wrist/end effector. Do NOT
   call it wrist just because a gripper is visible, the arm fills much of the
   image, the view is close to the manipulation area, the camera points roughly
   where the gripper points, or it is mounted above the table near the robot.

   Use the SEVERAL frames over time. A wrist camera shows EGOCENTRIC motion: when
   the arm changes pose the entire viewpoint changes with it; the background shows
   large viewpoint/parallax shifts correlated with the robot's motion; the gripper
   or wrist tends to stay at roughly the same place/orientation relative to the
   camera; the workspace appears to move AROUND the camera. A FIXED external camera
   is the opposite: the table, walls and background stay in the same camera frame;
   the robot moves THROUGH the image; the gripper may move a lot within the image;
   the scene's viewpoint stays geometrically fixed. If the background stays fixed
   while only the arm/gripper moves, the camera MUST NOT be classified as wrist.

   Decide in a strict order:
   1. Compare all frames chronologically; decide whether the camera itself moves
      with the robot (egocentric) or the scene viewpoint stays fixed.
   2. If it moves with the robot → wrist.
   3. If it stays fixed → an external camera: looking predominantly DOWN at the
      workspace → top (it need NOT be perfectly vertical — an elevated, mostly
      downward view is top even if the wrist/gripper is very prominent); looking
      UP from below → bottom; otherwise a view from around the robot at height →
      side, with a direction (front_side / rear_side / left_side / right_side, or
      plain "side" if you cannot tell).
   4. If this cannot be determined reliably, answer "unknown" rather than guess.

   WRIST HANDEDNESS. Do NOT infer left/right during view classification — first
   return plain "wrist". Add "left_wrist" / "right_wrist" ONLY with explicit,
   reliable evidence that the robot has distinct left and right manipulators AND
   this camera can be tied to one of them. Do NOT infer handedness from which side
   of the image the arm appears on, the gripper's orientation, mirroring, or an
   apparent left/right in a single frame. If handedness is uncertain, keep "wrist".

   {combo_rule}Put the direction first in a combo ("front_side", "left_wrist").
   left/right/front/rear are direction qualifiers — use them only as a suffix on
   side/wrist, never on their own. Do not invent words outside the vocabulary.

Output strictly valid JSON, no prose, no code fences, with exactly these keys:
