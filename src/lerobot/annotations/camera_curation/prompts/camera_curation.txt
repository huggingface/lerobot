You are inspecting frames from ONE camera of a robot manipulation dataset. All
frames come from the same fixed camera during a single episode; use them
together to judge the camera, not any single moment.

Do two things and return them as one JSON object.

1. QUALITY. Decide whether this camera view is usable for training a policy.
   Mark it UNUSABLE if EITHER:
   - it has a genuine capture defect: blurry / out of focus, badly over- or
     under-exposed, static/frozen, corrupted, or otherwise not showing the scene
     clearly; OR
   - a human is visible in the frame. These are teleoperated recordings and the
     human operator must never appear — any visible person, human hand, arm, or
     body part (as opposed to the robot) makes the view unusable.
   Otherwise it is usable.

   Do NOT mark a view unusable because the robot's own gripper, arm, or
   end-effector is visible or partially blocks the view — that is EXPECTED,
   especially for a wrist-mounted camera where the gripper is normally in frame.
   Carefully distinguish the robot (a metal/plastic manipulator) from a human
   limb: the robot occluding its own workspace is fine; a human in frame is not.
   Set "blur_reason" to the reason for any UNUSABLE verdict (e.g. "out of focus",
   "human operator visible").

2. VIEW LABEL. Choose the best label for where this camera sits relative to the
   robot, using ONLY this closed vocabulary:

     {vocabulary}

   This kind of robot arm has no obvious "face", so anchor "front" to its WORKING
   direction: the front is the side the arm reaches out toward to manipulate
   objects — the side the workspace and the manipulated objects are on. (No human
   is ever in these frames; do not use a person as a cue.) Then judge from what
   the camera actually shows:

   - front  : you see the robot from its working side, roughly head-on. The
              gripper and the manipulated objects face you, and the arm mostly
              moves TOWARD / AWAY from you (it looks foreshortened, not spread
              out across the frame).
   - rear   : you see the robot from behind — motor backs, mounting and cabling
              are the prominent features, and the arm reaches AWAY from you into
              a workspace you can barely see.
   - side   : you see the arm in PROFILE — its links span left-to-right across
              the frame and you can read its full reach sideways. ALWAYS qualify
              with which side of the robot the camera is on: "left_side" or
              "right_side".
   - top    : looking straight down on the workspace from above (overhead).
   - bottom : looking up at the workspace from below.
   - wrist  : mounted on the arm/gripper and moving with it — you see the gripper
              and the object close up and the viewpoint shifts as the arm moves;
              qualify with the arm when there are two — "left_wrist" / "right_wrist".

   Quick test for the front / rear / side confusion: if the arm's length runs
   LEFT-TO-RIGHT across the frame, it is a SIDE view; if the arm mainly points
   toward or away from you, it is FRONT (workspace facing you) or REAR (workspace
   away from you, back of the robot facing you).

   {combo_rule}When you combine a direction with a position, put the direction
   first ("left_side", "right_wrist"). Use "left"/"right" only as such a
   qualifier, never on their own. Do not invent words outside the vocabulary.

Output strictly valid JSON, no prose, no code fences, with exactly these keys:
