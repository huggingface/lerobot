You are jointly reviewing {n} camera streams from the SAME robot-manipulation
episode.

The streams are shown as "Camera 1", "Camera 2", etc. These identifiers are
ARBITRARY and contain NO information about camera position or mounting.

Each camera includes chronologically sampled frames.

For each camera you are also given the preliminary:

  - mount_type
  - view_label

from an independent per-camera analysis.

Treat the preliminary result as useful evidence but not absolute ground truth.

Your task is to use the joint comparison to produce the FINAL mount type and
view label for every camera.

Use ONLY this closed view-label vocabulary:

  {vocabulary}

Mount type must be one of:

  - "fixed"
  - "robot_mounted"
  - "unknown"

IMPORTANT:

Cameras are NOT required to have different labels.

Multiple cameras may legitimately be "top", "side", or "wrist".

NEVER change a label merely to make labels unique.

1. REVIEW MOUNT TYPE

First review the preliminary mount_type for each camera.

Do NOT change mount_type merely because another camera has the same mount type or
view label.

Change the preliminary mount_type ONLY when the visual evidence provides a clear
contradiction.

Static-environment test

Identify STATIC scene landmarks:

  - table/work-surface edges;
  - walls;
  - shelves;
  - fixtures;
  - stationary containers;
  - furniture;
  - background objects;
  - structural edges and corners.

Ignore the robot, gripper, manipulated objects, and humans for this test.

FIXED

A camera is "fixed" when static environment landmarks remain geometrically stable
across its frames.

The robot moves THROUGH the image while the static environment remains registered
to the camera.

A gripper may:

  - be very prominent;
  - stay visible across frames;
  - pass close to the lens;
  - occupy much of the image.

None of these facts makes the camera robot-mounted.

ROBOT_MOUNTED

Use "robot_mounted" ONLY with POSITIVE evidence of CAMERA motion.

The static environment itself must undergo global viewpoint/parallax changes as
the robot moves.

Strong evidence includes:

  - static landmarks substantially changing image position;
  - changes in perspective of static scene geometry;
  - global background motion correlated with robot-arm motion;
  - the workspace appearing to move around the camera.

Robot motion alone is NOT sufficient.

Object motion alone is NOT sufficient.

A close-up view alone is NOT sufficient.

A visible gripper alone is NOT sufficient.

If static background landmarks remain geometrically stable, the camera MUST be
"fixed".

If there is insufficient evidence to contradict a preliminary "fixed"
classification, KEEP "fixed".

Do not change "fixed" to "robot_mounted" based only on ambiguity.

2. ROBOT-MOUNTED CAMERAS

If mount_type == "robot_mounted", use:

  - "wrist"

Use "left_wrist" or "right_wrist" ONLY when explicit and reliable evidence ties
the camera to a distinct left or right manipulator.

Do NOT infer handedness from apparent image direction, gripper orientation,
mirroring, or which side of the image contains the robot.

When uncertain, use plain "wrist".

3. FIXED CAMERAS

For fixed cameras, classify according to PHYSICAL CAMERA POSITION relative to
the manipulation workspace.

top

Use "top" when the camera is physically positioned predominantly ABOVE the
manipulation workspace and looks predominantly downward.

It does NOT need to be perfectly vertical.

An elevated oblique camera can be "top" if its physical position is primarily
above the manipulation area.

Evidence includes:

  - substantial visibility of horizontal work surfaces;
  - objects seen substantially from above;
  - an overhead/global perspective;
  - relatively compressed depth along the work surface.

bottom

Use "bottom" when the camera is physically below the manipulation workspace and
looks upward.

side

Use a side label when the camera is physically positioned primarily OUTSIDE the
workspace boundary and looks INWARD.

A side camera can be elevated and can look downward.

Therefore downward viewing angle alone does NOT make a camera "top".

Side views generally show stronger depth perspective, more vertical surfaces,
and a more oblique view across the workspace.

When direction is reliably identifiable:

  - "front_side"
  - "rear_side"
  - "left_side"
  - "right_side"

Otherwise use "side".

Do NOT infer direction merely from where the robot appears within the image.

4. JOINT TOP-VS-SIDE COMPARISON

Use the other fixed cameras as RELATIVE geometric evidence.

For cameras that might be top or side, compare:

  - how much horizontal work surface is visible;
  - how strongly objects are viewed from above;
  - amount of vertical scene geometry visible;
  - perspective depth across the work surface;
  - apparent physical position relative to the workspace boundary.

Ask for each camera:

Is this camera primarily ABOVE the manipulation area, or primarily OUTSIDE its
boundary looking inward?

  - primarily ABOVE -> "top"
  - primarily OUTSIDE -> side

The camera providing the strongest overhead/global perspective is a strong "top"
candidate.

A camera viewing the same workspace primarily from outside its boundary should
remain a side view even when elevated and looking downward.

However, this comparison is evidence only.

Do NOT assume exactly one camera must be top.

Do NOT assume exactly one camera must be side.

Do NOT assign different labels merely because they are different cameras.

5. UNCERTAINTY AND SPECIFICITY

Use "unknown" instead of guessing when the category cannot be established
reliably.

Prefer a less-specific valid label over unsupported specificity:

  - "side" instead of guessing front/rear/left/right;
  - "wrist" instead of guessing left/right wrist.

6. CONSISTENCY

Valid combinations include:

  - fixed + top
  - fixed + bottom
  - fixed + side
  - fixed + front_side
  - fixed + rear_side
  - fixed + left_side
  - fixed + right_side
  - robot_mounted + wrist
  - robot_mounted + left_wrist
  - robot_mounted + right_wrist
  - unknown + unknown

A FIXED camera MUST NOT be labeled wrist.

A ROBOT_MOUNTED camera MUST NOT be labeled top, bottom, or side.

{combo_rule}

Do not invent labels outside the vocabulary.

Return strictly valid JSON with one entry per camera in exactly the same order as
