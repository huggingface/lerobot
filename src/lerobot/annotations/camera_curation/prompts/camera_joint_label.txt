Vocabulary: {vocabulary}. Mount type: fixed | robot_mounted | unknown.

DUPLICATES ARE NORMAL. Never change a label just to make the set unique, diverse,

1. MOUNT TYPE — change only on a clear contradiction.

robot_mounted needs POSITIVE evidence, from EITHER:
  A. viewpoint motion — the static world (table edges, walls, fixtures) globally
     re-frames / parallaxes correlated with robot motion (a wrist camera may also be
     still; on a blank backdrop track CONCRETE objects, not the blank background); OR
  B. own-gripper geometry — the camera's OWN gripper, belonging to its arm, emerges
     from the near edge and stays PINNED to the same image spot across frames (you
     look OUT past it). Need not open or drift; this OVERRIDES a static/blank
     backdrop.

fixed: static landmarks stay registered while the robot moves through; OR you look
AT a separate, external arm (joints/servos seen from the outside, even a close one);
OR a big arm merely SWINGS through a stationary scene. A close/prominent gripper
ALONE is not enough — a wrist's own gripper is pinned to the EXACT same spot across
frames, while a fixed camera's gripper travels. If unresolved, KEEP the preliminary
mount; do not flip on ambiguity, and never resolve a label collision by changing
fixed <-> robot_mounted.

2. ROBOT_MOUNTED -> "wrist". If TWO cameras are robot_mounted they are the robot's
two hands: differentiate left_wrist / right_wrist from HAND ANATOMY (palm/back +
thumb side — a left and right hand are mirror images), never from image side or the
gripper's pointing direction. Fall back to plain "wrist" for a 2-jaw gripper or when
the hand cannot be read.

3. FIXED -> top / bottom / side.

TOP vs SIDE: camera ABOVE the workspace looking DOWN onto it (laid out below the
lens, tops of things / into boxes) -> "top"; camera at table/eye level looking
ACROSS it (objects edge-on, arms standing up) -> "side". LEAN TOP: an elevated
downward camera is "top" even at a strong diagonal, with perspective, a wall behind,
or object fronts; it need not be near-nadir. WHEN IN DOUBT, choose TOP. Do NOT demote
a genuine overhead just because another camera is more overhead, and do NOT promote a
clearly across / lateral camera to "top" just because it is the highest one.
BOTTOM: camera below the workspace looking up.

SIDE DIRECTION (optional — plain "side" is valid). The directions say where the
CAMERA physically sits relative to the robot. Find the robot's FRONT (working
direction / where the objects and active workspace are; motor-backs and cabling mark
the BACK — objects are stronger evidence):
  - front_side: objects between robot and camera, arm reaching TOWARD you;
    rear_side: workspace beyond the robot / motor-backs toward you, arm reaching AWAY.
  - left / right — from robot geometry, using body position AND reach TOGETHER (never
    the image side alone): body toward image RIGHT + reaching LEFT -> left_side; body
    toward image LEFT + reaching RIGHT -> right_side. Judge EACH camera independently
    — two side cameras need NOT be an opposite pair; the SAME layout gives the SAME
    label. If the base is out of frame, ambiguous, or possibly mirrored -> plain
    "side".

4. CONSISTENCY. Valid: fixed + top/bottom/side/front_side/rear_side/left_side/
right_side; robot_mounted + wrist/left_wrist/right_wrist; unknown + unknown. A FIXED
camera is NEVER wrist; a ROBOT_MOUNTED camera is NEVER top/bottom/side. Prefer
a less-specific valid label (side over a guessed direction, wrist over a guessed
