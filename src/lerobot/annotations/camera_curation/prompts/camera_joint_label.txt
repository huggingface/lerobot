Critical wrist-vs-fixed rule. Classify a camera as "wrist" ONLY when there is
strong evidence it is physically mounted on the moving arm/wrist/end effector. Do
NOT call it wrist just because a gripper is visible, the arm fills much of the
image, the view is close to the manipulation area, the camera points roughly
where the gripper points, or it is mounted above the table near the robot. A wrist
camera shows EGOCENTRIC motion across its own frames: when the arm changes pose
the whole viewpoint changes with it, the background shows large viewpoint/parallax
shifts correlated with the robot's motion, and the gripper/wrist stays at roughly
the same place relative to the camera. A FIXED external camera is the opposite:
the table/walls/background stay in the same camera frame while the robot moves
THROUGH the image (the gripper may still move a lot within the frame). If the
background stays fixed while only the arm/gripper moves, it MUST NOT be wrist. In
particular, a FIXED camera looking predominantly DOWN at the workspace is "top"
even if the wrist/gripper is very prominent (it need not be perfectly vertical —
an elevated, mostly-downward view is top).

Wrist handedness. Do NOT infer left/right while classifying the view — return
plain "wrist" first. Add "left_wrist" / "right_wrist" ONLY with explicit, reliable
evidence that the robot has distinct left and right manipulators AND this camera
ties to one of them. Do NOT infer handedness from which side of the image the arm
appears on, gripper orientation, mirroring, or an apparent left/right in a single
frame. If handedness is uncertain, keep "wrist".

Camera mounts may be TILTED or ROTATED; judge each view from the robot's and the
work surface's orientation, mentally leveling the image — not from the frame
edges. The cameras of one setup almost always point from DIFFERENT places, so use
the side-by-side comparison to give each a DISTINCT label; only repeat a label if
two cameras genuinely share the same viewpoint. If a camera's viewpoint is
genuinely ambiguous, answer "unknown" rather than guessing.
